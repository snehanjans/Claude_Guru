# Great Learning Ambassadors — program context

Everything a fresh instance needs to know about **the referral program itself** before
building a landing page for it. This is a description of the program, not of the app.

Numbers here are transcribed from the prototype's own data, so a page built from this
document will agree with what the dashboard shows.

---

## 1. What the program is

**Great Learning Ambassadors** is an invitation-only referral program for people who
already mentor for Great Learning ("**Gurus**"). A Guru recommends a GL program to
someone in their own professional network, and earns a bonus when that person enrols.

Two things define its character:

- **The audience is not the public.** Every Ambassador is already a paid GL mentor, so
  they have standing with learners and a track record to point at. The pitch is
  "monetise the credibility you already have", not "join an affiliate scheme".
- **The Guru never sells or supports.** Great Learning owns the sales conversation,
  the refunds and every learner complaint. The Guru's job ends at the introduction.

The internal framing is a *collective* of mentors, not a sales channel.

---

## 2. How a Guru earns — two different models

This is the most important mechanic on the page, and it is **not one rate**.

### GL "AI-Native" programs → percentage of fees

| Path | Guru earns |
|---|---|
| Learner enrols **on their own** (self-checkout) | **20%** of collected fees |
| A GL **learning consultant** helps them decide (assisted) | **10%** of collected fees |

- "Collected fees" means what the learner actually paid, **after** any discount.
- Paid **after the refund window closes**.
- The doubling for self-checkout is deliberate: the Guru who does the whole job of
  convincing someone keeps twice as much as one who hands off a lead.

### University programs → flat bonus

- **$150 / ₹15,000 per enrolment**, regardless of the program's price.
- Paid **one month after course start**.
- These are refer-only; the Guru does not mentor on them.

The practical consequence: a $999 GL program can pay more than a $5,000 university
program. Percentage beats flat here, which is counter-intuitive and worth stating
plainly rather than letting someone assume the expensive program pays better.

## 3. The catalogue — 8 programs

### GL AI-Native family — 4 programs, percentage model

All four: **6 weeks**, **Mentored**, **$999 / ₹50,000**, **no coding required**, all
flagged as new. Same 20%/10% split, same payout timing, each with its own promo code.

| Program | Audience | One-liner |
|---|---|---|
| AI-Native Professional | general professionals | Automate everyday work with AI agents |
| AI-Native **HR** Professional | HRBP, TA, L&D | Automate recurring HR work with AI |

Curriculum is consistent in shape: AI foundations and prompting → domain-specific

## 4. What the learner gets

Every program carries a **promo code giving the learner a discount** — 20% on the GL
AI-Native programs, 12–18% on the university ones.

The code is **per program, not per Guru**: it is the same code whoever shares it. It
is the Guru's sweetener to offer, not a personal asset.

There is a program variant where the Guru does **not** hold a code to hand out, and
the learner instead claims the discount shown on the program page. Copy should not
assume the Guru personally "gives" the discount.

---

## 5. How a referral is credited

Each Guru has a **personalised program page** per program. Anything that enrols
through that page is credited to them automatically. There is nothing to submit and
no form to fill.

Two identifiers exist and are easy to confuse:

- **The Guru's referral ID** — identifies *who* referred. Rides on the share link.
- **The program's promo code** — gives the *learner* their discount.

Attribution is by link, so a Guru can post anywhere: WhatsApp, LinkedIn, email, or
any channel their network uses. Credit holds whether the learner enrols directly or
first books a call with a learning consultant.

## 6. The referral lifecycle

Seven states. Five are the happy path; two are dead ends.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sent** | Guru has shared the link / submitted the contact |
| **GL reached out** | a learning consultant has made contact |
| **Enrolled** | learner has signed up for a cohort |
| **Payout confirmed** | the reward is locked in and owed |
| **Paid** | money has reached the Guru |
| *Not eligible* | disqualified — see rules below |
| *Not converted* | GL spoke to them; no enrolment |

Status is **not real-time** — it refreshes roughly every 6 hours. A page should not
promise instant tracking.

Realistic reasons a referral fails, taken from the prototype's own examples: budget
approval didn't come through; chose a cheaper self-paced alternative; went quiet after
pricing; already an active GL lead.

---

## 7. Eligibility rules — the fine print that matters

- The referral must be a **new or inactive** prospective learner.
- **A referral is not eligible if GL already engaged that person in the previous
  2 months** through its own outreach, or if GL counsellors were already supporting
  their decision. This is the single most likely cause of an unexpected zero payout
  and should not be buried.
- If a learner **drops out and takes a refund**, no reward is paid.
- If a learner pays in **instalments**, the reward lands once the full fee is paid.

---

## 8. When money actually arrives

The reward is confirmed once the referral has *enrolled, joined their cohort, and
paid the full fee* — typically about **3 weeks after cohort start**. Confirmed rewards
are then paid **monthly, alongside the Guru's normal mentoring payments**.

So the honest end-to-end expectation is on the order of **1–2 months from enrolment
to cash**, not instant. University programs state it differently again (one month
after course start).

---

## 9. Currency

Learners pay in **their own** currency, so bonuses are earned in whatever the learner
paid. A Guru's totals are then converted into **their own payout currency** for
display, at a fixed rate in the prototype.

Consequence for any page showing earnings: the same referral is "$200" or "₹16,800"
depending on who is looking. Pick one currency per view and be consistent; do not mix
them in a single total.

## 10. What Great Learning provides

Worth listing on a landing page, because it answers "what do I actually have to do?":

- A **personalised program page** per program, pre-tagged to the Guru.
- **Ready-made collateral** in four formats — LinkedIn post, WhatsApp broadcast,
  email intro, Instagram story — with promo-code and code-free variants.
- A **discount code** for their network.
- **Guru-run webinars**: a Guru can host a marketing webinar for a GL program using a
  GL-owned deck they personalise. Registrations are attributed to them. The prototype
  shows real scale — one completed webinar had **214 registered, 131 attended**.
- A **tracking dashboard** covering every referral from first contact to payout.
- **All learner support, complaints and refunds handled by GL.**

The share messages are templated with placeholders — the Guru's name, their number of
learners mentored, the audience, the discount, the code — so a Guru personalises
rather than writes from scratch.

## 12. Discrepancies and open questions

Flagging these because a landing page states numbers confidently and these are the
places where it would state something wrong.

1. **"20%" is a rate, not an amount.** 20% of a $999 program is $200 — but the learner
   usually applies a 20% discount first, so the collected fee is ~$799 and the real
   bonus is **~$160**. The prototype's own sample referrals use the round $200/₹10,000
   figures, which are optimistic. Decide deliberately whether the page leads with the
   *rate* (safe) or an *illustrative amount* (needs the discount factored in).
2. **Two payout stories coexist** — "after the refund window closes" for GL programs
   versus "one month after course start" for university ones. A single headline
   payout promise will be wrong for one of them.
3. **Assisted referrals earn half.** Any earnings illustration should say which path
   it assumes, or a Guru will feel short-changed at 10%.
4. **Learner discount varies by program** (20% GL, 12–18% university). "Your network
   gets 20% off" is only true for the GL family.

### The one question that must be answered before building

**Who is the landing page for?** These are three different pages:

- **A Guru not yet in Ambassadors** → lead with earning potential and low effort.
- **A Guru already in it who hasn't referred anyone** → lead with the mechanics:
  where the link is, what to send, what happens next.
- **A learner arriving from a referral link** → lead with the program and the
  discount; the referral economics are irrelevant and should not appear at all.

Audience determines structure, so resolve this first.

---

## 13. Tone

Program copy should read as **statements, not instructions**. Say what happens rather
than telling the Guru what to do, and reserve "please" for genuine asks. Avoid
internal jargon — say "the team" or "a learning consultant", never "ops"; spell out
"learning consultant" rather than "LC".
